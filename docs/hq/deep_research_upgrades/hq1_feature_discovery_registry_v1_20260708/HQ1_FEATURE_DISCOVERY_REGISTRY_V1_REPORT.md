## Major Source Findings

The most useful public foundation remains nflverse: play-by-play, player stats, team stats, rosters, schedules, injuries, participation, and crosswalks. These sources still require the existing NWR source gates, point-in-time rules, identity discipline, and missingness controls before any future scoring lane uses them.

PFR advanced stats and PFR snap counts are worth review because they may provide public role, efficiency, snap, and position context. They remain review-only until future lanes prove stable ingestion, historical coverage, identity joins through PFR/nflverse keys, and source semantics.

NGS public aggregates and FTN public charting subsets are worth tracking as review-only or display-only surfaces. Their limitations are metric-specific coverage, historical gaps, source semantics, and reproducibility. They are not promoted here.

PFF, SIS, Sportradar, commercial FTN, and scraped proprietary route data remain blocked unless a future source-admission lane establishes license rights, reproducible access, field semantics, identity joins, coverage, and leakage controls.

## Route/YPRR/TPRR Status

True YPRR and TPRR remain blocked because they require full routes-run denominators. nflverse participation primary-receiver route labels are useful context but are not full routes-run denominators. Public route/YPRR surfaces may exist on the open web, but this lane found no approved broad public reproducible join-safe feed.

Allowed route-like proxies are preserved separately in `HQ1_ROUTE_PROXY_METRIC_BACKLOG.csv`. These proxies include team-pass-attempt denominators, target share, first-down conversion, air-yards context, and snap-based proxies only when the snap source is safe. They must never be labeled as true route metrics.

## Validation Methodology Summary

Future tournaments should use frozen candidate, baseline, target, and split registries. Walk-forward season splits are required. N-to-N+1 is the primary target structure. No random splits should be used. Position-specific baselines are required because QB, RB, WR, TE, and cross-position dynasty/meta signals have different replacement levels and error shapes.

Missingness and coverage bias must be tracked as first-class outputs. Leave-one-season-out checks and outlier audits should be part of review-only validation. New metrics must not be tested by dropping them into the current production formula. GREEN means review-only signal, not source, feature, or production promotion.
