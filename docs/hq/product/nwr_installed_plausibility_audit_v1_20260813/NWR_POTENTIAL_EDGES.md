# NWR potential edges

These are supported workflow/model advantages, not claims of proven predictive alpha.

## League-specific position economics

The installed Dynasty board and Trade Analyzer consistently apply 10-team 1QB scarcity. Generic Superflex-influenced market lists can overstate the value of ordinary QB depth. This is a practical edge when the owner's league truly behaves like a shallow 1QB league.

## Evidence honesty

Confidence caps, unavailable route fields, unknown pick slots, and blocked rookie identities remain visible. NWR is more useful than a rank-only consensus source when the decision depends on knowing what is *not* supported.

## Multi-horizon decisions

Player Compare and Outcomes distinguish current production from medium/long-horizon value. The audit found directionally coherent shifts for veterans, young breakouts, and team-window trade changes.

## Rookie evidence separation

Draft capital, production, identity, and confidence are reviewed separately from the finished veteran scale. The system correctly avoids pretending that a rookie review score is directly comparable to a veteran production score.

## Market disagreement discovery

The sign-correct market lens quickly surfaces players for research without forcing NWR to follow consensus. This can expose useful buy-low questions when the underlying NWR evidence is fresh and complete.

## Explainable trade decomposition

Even where the final label is coarse, the ten decision dimensions reveal *why* the system prefers a side and which change moved the result. This is more actionable than an opaque calculator total.

## Candidate independent-signal cluster

NWR's willingness to elevate efficient/explosive receivers with uncertain generic-market roles (for example Alec Pierce, Jameson Williams, Michael Wilson, and Parker Washington) may be a useful research edge. It is **not yet historically validated**; treat it as a shortlist generator and track outcomes prospectively.
